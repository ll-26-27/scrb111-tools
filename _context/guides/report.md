# Writing the overview for the report

*November 24, 2026. This file is the professor's to rewrite: the report skill reads all of it every time.*

The overview sits at the top of `outputs/report.html`, above the videos. The professor reads it before the class in which the videos are shown and the questions are asked, so it should tell her what the set looks like as a whole and what is worth raising with everyone.

## Summary

One short paragraph: how many videos, which topics in stem cell and regenerative biology they take up, and how they go about explaining them to a general audience (analogies, drawings, props, animation, talking to camera).

## What comes up more than once (`themes`)

Three to six. Each is something that appears in two or more videos, said specifically enough to act on. The places to look are the same ones the follow-up questions come from:

- a term several videos use without defining it, or define differently
- a step of mechanism that several videos pass over (how something gets into a cell, how it finds its target, what recognizes what)
- a limitation that several videos name in a phrase and move on from: delivery, specificity, safety, cost, ethics
- a claim of the same shape made across videos ("safer," "reversible," "personalized") that would be worth comparing
- a way of explaining, such as a kind of analogy, that several pairs chose

Name the videos under each one. Something found in a single video belongs on that video's page, not here.

## To raise with the whole class (`for_class`)

Two to five questions that several videos lead to, written the way the follow-up questions are written (`_context/guides/questions.md`): short, one part, open, in plain words, no praise. These come from the themes; they are not a second list of questions for individual pairs.

## Keep to

- Description. No ranking, no grades, no rubric language, no best and worst.
- What the transcripts and stills support. If a theme rests on a term the transcriber may have garbled, say so.
- Videos named by file name, not by the students' names, unless the professor asks otherwise.
