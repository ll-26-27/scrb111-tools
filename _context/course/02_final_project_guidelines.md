# Final project

The final project is a semester-long effort between a small group of students (projected to be pairs of students, but if course enrollment reaches a certain threshold, we are open to considering groups of three). Students will choose their own partners/teams. The final presentation should not exceed ~5 minutes in length. The presentation should center on a key concept in stem cell and regenerative biology, preferably one with immediate relevance and impact in current events. The target audience is the general public, so students will need to be sure to define all terms and to clearly articulate methods in simple terms. The audience should be assumed to not have any specialized training in science; beware of jargon and try to distill the concepts. We expect students to choose an important topic and to veer away from those for which outstanding videos aimed at the general public already exist—use this opportunity to create content that the world needs! Important points:

• Students will be provided a detailed description of the goals and format of the final presentation project. They will also be given a grading rubric.

• This year, we will be working with the Bok Center to develop guidelines for use of ChatGPT as part of the assignment. Students will be expected to use ChatGPT in developing their ideas, and we will ask groups to hand in their conversations with ChatGPT to show how they further developed themes and strategies for communicating ideas using this tool. We will use Chat GPT to produce questions based on student videos and we will ask students these questions after their presentations in class.

• Each student in the group is expected to contribute to planning and presenting.

• The presentations will be videotaped in collaboration with the Bok Center and they may be hosted on an online portal accessible by the general public in the future.

We will have a “viewing party” in our final class meeting
